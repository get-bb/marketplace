## What you get

- One continuous petroleum-blue field, anchored to the window and shared by every large surface, so the composition does not restart at each pane's corner.
- Dark and light as the same room at two exposures: the same bloom low and centre, the same texture, the same hierarchy — the light changes, the design does not.
- Matching code, diff, file-tree, and terminal colours through bundled Pierre-compatible light and dark themes.
- IBM Plex Sans for conversation and navigation, with IBM Plex Mono for code, timestamps, and operational metadata.
- An illuminated hairline seam on the focused pane, so focus is visible without a border or a pulse.

## How it behaves

E Corp follows bb's light and dark mode setting automatically. It includes dedicated focus, selection, warning, success, destructive, diff, and ANSI terminal colours rather than flattening semantic states into the neutral palette. Every text tier is measured against the darkest and lightest points the field reaches in that mode.

The fonts and the texture are embedded in the stylesheet, so the theme is self-contained and makes no network requests. Reduced-motion and reduced-transparency preferences are supported. The theme adds no commands, background services, settings, accounts, API keys, or external dependencies.

After installation, select **E Corp** under Settings → Appearance, or run `bb theme set plugin:e-corp:e-corp`.

## Where it comes from

E Corp is named for the conglomerate in *Mr. Robot*, and its field is read from one image in the series: Elliot Alderson's desktop wallpaper. The composition is taken from it — light low and slightly right of centre, shade at the top edge and outer corners — along with the petroleum-blue register the palette is built on. The texture embedded in the stylesheet is a high-passed greyscale derivative of the same image, so the mottling across every panel is the wallpaper's own. That shared origin is why light mode is an exposure of the field rather than an inversion of it.
